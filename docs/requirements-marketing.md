# Marketing requirements — draft, lecturer approval outstanding

Client: Sam, marketing manager of a grocery and wine retailer, allocating a limited contact budget.

1. Who are our best customers: loyal, buying often, never complaining? [remember]
2. Group our customers into a handful of types I can name and plan for. [remember]
3. Before we spend money calling this customer, guess whether they will say yes to the next campaign. [guess]
4. For a new sign-up, guess how often they will shop with us in store, online and by catalog. [guess]
5. Do families with teenagers really shop differently? [certify]
6. Do our big wine buyers really respond to campaigns more than everyone else? [certify]
7. Each time a new customer signs up, tell me whether to send them a discount coupon. [flow]
8. Tell me when you've seen enough new sign-ups that your advice stops changing. [flow]
9. What do the customers who ignored our last campaign have in common? [blame]
10. Why do some people visit the website a lot but hardly ever buy? [blame]
11. When you say a customer is not worth contacting, explain why in plain words. [justify]
12. When you recommend contacting a customer, show me similar customers and whether they responded. [justify]
13. Find the customers most likely to respond, while looking up as few past responses as possible. [choose]
14. For our new family campaign, find the customers with kids at home who are most likely to respond. [choose]
15. These customers haven't bought anything in over two months. What is the smallest difference between them and similar customers who still respond to us? [fix]
16. These customers mostly buy on discount. What is the smallest difference between them and similar customers who mostly pay full price? [fix]
17. Spot customer records that are probably typos, such as strange incomes or negative spending. [spot]
18. Which customers spend far more, or far less, than people with similar income and family? [spot]
19. We could just contact our richest customers. Is your advice any better than that? [race]
20. Right now we contact the customers who spent the most and said yes to the most past campaigns. Does your advice find more responders than that? [race]

## Feasibility

- This dataset supports observed campaign-response prediction, not demonstrated future retention.
- Association and similar-customer contrasts do not establish causation.
- Coupon decisions require assumptions; response prediction alone does not estimate coupon effectiveness.
- New-sign-up predictions need an input schema excluding unavailable purchase history.
- The initial implementation covers only the asks actually demonstrated.
